# THESIS (v0, claim locked 2026-10-31 13:20Z)

## The claim

**NEAR's long-term investment case is a bet that NEAR Intents becomes the default settlement layer for cross-chain money moved by software, including autonomous agents.**

We test three halves of that bet separately. We expect them to come out differently:

1. **Usage.** NEAR's activity has changed form. Count metrics (transactions, active accounts) collapsed, while value settled through NEAR Intents grew by orders of magnitude and now dominates NEAR's trading volume.
2. **Value capture.** That usage does **not yet** accrue to the NEAR token in a measurable way: protocol fees are small next to Intents' own fees, and NEAR's price does not move with Intents volume.
3. **Agents.** Agent-native demand on NEAR (the NEAR AI Agent Market) is real but still tiny relative to Intents. A live autonomous agent's own money flow shows what share of an agent's activity is the cross-chain kind that Intents is built for.

An investment thesis that is judged on evidence has to say which half is proven and which is hope. That is what the tests below decide.

> **Correction (2026-10-31 ~21:40Z, after the lock).** Half 2 above is wrong as written. A NEAR Intents fee switch went live on **2026-02-23**: since then, NEAR's captured share of Intents fees buys NEAR on the open market. Our own falsifier for this half ("a documented mechanism routes Intents fees to NEAR holders", below) was therefore already met when we locked the claim, and we missed it. In the 90 days to 2026-10-30 the buyback took **27.6% of Intents' fees ($4.7M)**. Measured against the token, it is small: about **0.35% of market cap a year**, and about **13% of the NEAR that net issuance adds**. The claim text stays as locked; T3 stays as registered (it counts protocol fees only, and fails); the new test **C1** measures the buyback. Details in "Findings added after the lock".

## What we looked at before locking (disclosure)

On 2026-10-31, before writing this file, we explored:
- DefiLlama's NEAR Intents and NEAR-chain series;
- NearBlocks' daily charts.

One exploratory result is already known and is reported as **evidence against**, not hidden:
- Weekly log-changes of NEAR price show **no correlation** with weekly log-changes of NEAR Intents volume (r ≈ 0.00), active accounts (≈ −0.05) or transactions (≈ 0.00), over 94 weeks from 2025-01-06.
- In levels, price vs Intents volume is negatively related (r ≈ −0.49), because Intents grew through 2025 while price fell.
- We therefore do **not** claim that price follows Intents.

**T8 extension (2026-10-31 ~19:40Z): the agent's own NEAR account is counted too.** The 1Click move above created a NEAR account for the agent. From 19:18Z that account sends transactions of its own, and T8 now counts them in its denominator. NEAR has no `eth_getTransactionCount`, so the count is read from the account's access-key nonce over public RPC. A new key's nonce starts at a round multiple of 10⁶ fixed when the key is added, and each included transaction adds 1, so sent = nonce mod 10⁶, summed over the account's keys. NearBlocks' transaction count for the account is recorded next to it as a cross-check. On 10-31 both read 3 (one failed with "Not enough storage balance", and as on EVM, a failed transaction still counts as sent). None of them is cross-chain, so T8's cross-chain count is unchanged and the share moves from 3/498 to 3/501 (0.6% either way). **Disclosure:** every one of these NEAR transactions is the companion project, run by the same agent, writing its records to SocialDB (`social.near`). They show that the agent can use NEAR directly, but they are not independent evidence of agent demand on NEAR, and T6/T7 do not use them.

**T5 note (2026-10-31 ~20Z): the counter method reads an estimate, and a third method agrees with the RPC sample.** The `intents.near` count that T5's counter method takes deltas of is, by NearBlocks' own API spec, an estimated count. Its hourly deltas therefore track the estimate, not traffic, which explains the low ~4%. NearBlocks also publishes daily per-account statistics (`/v3/accounts/intents.near/stats/txns`). Divided by NearBlocks' daily network transactions, they give 15% for 2026-10-24 → 10-30 (12–21% by day), against 14% in our own RPC block samples (784 transactions over 9 samples). T5 now reports the daily-stats share and the RPC-sample share as its measurements, and the counter share under a name that says it is estimated. T5 has no threshold, so no verdict changes.

**Fee switch (found 2026-10-31 ~21:40Z): value capture exists, through buybacks.** While checking our capture falsifier before publishing, we found that NEAR Intents activated a fee switch on 2026-02-23. Captured Intents revenue is swept into NEAR's revenue wallets and buys NEAR on the open market. DefiLlama publishes it as the `dailyHoldersRevenue` series of `near-intents` ("NEAR's captured Intents revenue is used to buy back $NEAR on the open market (not burned)"; DefiLlama says the series reconciles with revenue.near.org and the on-chain buyback). The bought-back NEAR sits in `buybacks.multisignature.near`, which held 2.14M NEAR on 10-31 by RPC `view_account`, so total supply still counts it. **C1** (REPORT, not scored, added after the lock) puts it next to the token:
- over the same 90 days as T3: buyback revenue ÷ Intents fees, and (buyback + NEAR's gas burn) annualized ÷ market cap (NearBlocks `/v1/stats`, circulating supply × price);
- NEAR's net issuance: total supply from two archival block headers about 90 days apart (it is already net of the gas burn), annualized by the headers' own timestamps, and the annualized buyback ÷ that issuance in USD at NearBlocks' price.

On 10-31: buyback 27.6% of Intents fees; captured value 0.35% of market cap a year; net issuance 2.5% of total supply a year (≈ $143M); buyback ≈ 13% of it. NEAR's Q3 2026 report gives a trailing 30-day capture rate of 28.4% (as reported by [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/36730)), consistent with DefiLlama's 27.6% over 90 days. T3 is unchanged and still FAILs on its own measure: it asked whether *protocol fees* capture the value, and they do not. T4 (price vs volume, r ≈ 0) covers the buyback months too.

**P1, registered 2026-10-31 ~23:10Z before its sample was drawn: how large are the intents NEAR Intents executes?** T8 and T9 rest on one agent. P1 asks how much of all Intents activity is the size such an agent moves. `sample-intents.mjs` picks about 300 block heights at random over the 7 days before it runs (seeded by the head height, which it records), reads every transaction sent to `intents.near` in those blocks over public archival RPC, and sizes each executed intent in USD at 1Click's token prices at run time. Sizes are reported per kind and never merged:
- **transfer**: the 1Click path, where a user's tokens move to a solver;
- **token_diff**: swap legs, sized by the side the account gives (a swap shows a user leg and a solver leg);
- **withdraw**: tokens leaving Intents.
The fixed cutoffs are **agent-size: under $50** (the agent's own tickets were $0.47 to $2.02) and **under $10**. For each kind P1 reports n, the share under each cutoff and the median, and counts unpriced legs instead of guessing them. It is a REPORT with no threshold. "Agent-size" describes a ticket, not who sent it: chain data cannot tell agents from people (see Data limits).

**P1 result (2026-11-01 ~00:10Z).** The sample drawn from 22:01Z to 00:09Z: 300 random blocks over the 7 days to 2026-10-31, 1,103 transactions, 163 of them to `intents.near`, 357 executed intents, 2 legs unpriced.
- **Swap legs** (`token_diff`, n = 98): 48% under $50, 26% under $10, median $65.
- **Withdrawals** (n = 55): 47% under $50, 21% under $10, median $68.
- **Transfers** (n = 204): 85% under $50, 79% under $10, median $0.29.
The transfer result made us look closer, and this check came *after* seeing the result. We re-read 14 of the sampled blocks: 24 of 30 transfers there went to a few recurring implicit accounts, median $0.01. They are fee or affiliate payouts inside the same transactions, not users' tickets to solvers. So the registered "transfer" kind does not measure ticket size, and we read P1 from swap legs and withdrawals: **about half of what NEAR Intents executes is agent-size (under $50), and a fifth to a quarter is under $10.** Small tickets are not an edge case, which is why T9's per-route minimum matters. The same re-read also saw the NEAR AI Agent Market's escrow (`escrow.ai.near` → `treasury.ai.near`) settle through `intents.near`, so agent commerce already uses Intents for settlement. We did not measure how much.

**T4m, T4 with the crypto market taken out (added 2026-11-05, after the same comparison: the registered T4 is a raw correlation with no p-value and no market control).** We regress NEAR's weekly log price change on BTC's (BTC daily price from DefiLlama's coins API, `data/raw/defillama-btc-price.json`) and correlate what is left with Intents volume's weekly log change, over the same 95 weeks as T4. On 2026-11-05: BTC explained 34% of NEAR's weekly moves (beta 1.42), and the remainder did not track Intents volume (r = −0.03, two-sided permutation p = 0.79, 10,000 shuffles, fixed seed; the raw r was −0.004, p = 0.97). So T4's reading survives the control: in this window NEAR's price is not paid for by Intents usage, with or without the market. Limits: weekly changes are not perfectly independent, which weakens the permutation test's assumption; one control (BTC) only. The results block regenerates these numbers on every build. This is a REPORT line: T4's registered rule and verdict are unchanged. *Added 2026-11-06, after an independent review asked for both:* (1) a 4-week **block-permutation p**, which shuffles whole 4-week blocks so short-range serial correlation survives the shuffle (full window, at the 2026-11-06 builds: about 0.75, close to the plain 0.79); (2) the same test on the weeks **since the 2026-02-23 fee switch**, the break point C1 already uses (36 weeks, at the 2026-11-06 builds: r ≈ +0.25, plain and block p ≈ 0.15; not significant). The reviewer had computed (2) before we did (r +0.24, p ≈ 0.16), so it is not a blind test, and we report it with no threshold. A positive r after the fee switch is what the capture thesis would predict, but 36 weeks cannot tell it from noise. The current numbers are in **Status now**.

**T4L, T4m at lags of up to 8 weeks (added 2026-11-09, after the lock; a REPORT, not scored).** T4 and T4m compare the same week only. Two other entries in this track asked the obvious next question, whether one series moves *before* the other (NearBI: 7 of 144 lead-lag tests significant against 7.2 expected by chance; Erfan's thesis: a family-wise band over lags −8…+8). T4L takes T4m's BTC-controlled residual and correlates it with Intents volume's weekly log change at every lag from −8 to +8 weeks. A positive lag means volume moves first. For the chance level, the volume series is shifted around a circle by every offset that cannot bring a real link back inside the ±8 window (62 shifts over 96 weeks). Each shift keeps volume's own week-to-week pattern. For each shift we take the strongest |r| over all 17 lags, and the family-wise p is the share of shifts whose strongest |r| reaches ours. This is exact: no random seed, but also no p below 1/63 ≈ 0.016. On 2026-11-09: **Intents volume does not lead NEAR's price at any lag from 1 to 8 weeks** (strongest r = −0.09, at +7, against a chance band of 0.21). One lag does clear the band: r = −0.27, with NEAR's own price move leading volume by 3 weeks (p = 0.016, the floor of this test). It is negative and isolated (its neighbours are −0.06 and −0.09). It survives trimming each series at its 5th and 95th percentiles (r = −0.25). It does **not** survive ranks: Spearman gives −0.23 against a rank band of 0.28, p = 0.32. We read it as fragile and draw nothing from it. We ran the rank and trimming checks after seeing the −0.27, so they are disclosed checks, not a plan. Code: `lib/market.mjs` `leadLag`, tests in `test/market.test.mjs`. The status block below carries the current numbers.

**P2, who signs NEAR's transactions (added 2026-11-09, after the lock; a REPORT, not scored).** Account metrics such as NEAR's "active accounts" count the account that signs each NEAR transaction. Two things suggested that this is often not the user. In NEAR Intents, a relayer signs the transaction and the intent inside it carries the user's own signature. Another entry in this track (NearBI) traced most of the spring 2026 fall in NEAR's counts to one app, KaiChing. `sample-accounts.mjs` reads public blocks from neardata.xyz, one call per block, and has two parts:

- **The last 7 days:** about 300 random blocks before a recorded head. It counts every transaction's signer and, in the same blocks, every `dip4` event `intents.near` logged. Each such event names the account whose intent ran.
- **Spring 2026:** the same signer count on 150 random blocks in each of two fixed weeks, 2026-03-09..16 and 2026-04-13..20, grouped by parent account (`x.kaiching` → `kaiching`; a named `.near`/`.tg` account is its own parent).

A parent is named only if it is among a sample's 12 largest and has at least 5 transactions and 1% of the sample. Below that it could be one person, so it is counted but not listed.

The first run used the archival RPC (about 38 calls a block). It stopped on the last week when both archival endpoints refused further calls, and saved nothing. We moved to neardata and pinned the first part to that run's head. The new run gives the old run's transaction counts at every logged checkpoint: 1,353 transactions, 284 to `intents.near`, 7 signers, and 5,049 in the March week. Intent accounts come from receipts executed in the same blocks rather than traced per transaction, so their count is a sample of the same thing, not the same set.

**Result (sampled 2026-11-09).** In 300 blocks, 284 transactions went to `intents.near` (21% of all 1,353, between T5's two readings). They were signed by **7 accounts**, and the top 3 signed 99%: accounts under `intents.near` signed 248, under NEAR AI's `ai.near` 30 (the file keeps the parent, not the exact signer). In the same blocks `intents.near` logged 726 `dip4` event entries naming **166 distinct accounts** (154 implicit, 9 named, 3 0x). One swap logs several entries (a user leg and a solver leg, P1), so 726 is not a count of intents, and the 166 include solvers, so it is not a count of users. 3 of the 166 signed any sampled transaction. The two sets are not matched transaction by transaction. What the rows show is narrower: almost none of the accounts that NEAR Intents' events name appear as signers, so a signer-based account count leaves them out.

In spring, NEAR carried 33.7 transactions a block in the March week and 6.3 in the April week. One parent, `kaiching`, signed 3,967 of the 5,049 March transactions (26.4 a block, from 2 signing accounts) and dropped out of the April list (the 12 largest parents; the 12th had 12 transactions), so at most 0.08 a block. That is 96.5–96.8% of the drop. We then spot-checked one March block (188,755,667), chosen after seeing this result; it shows the same pattern as Intents. All 206 KaiChing transactions in it were signed by one account, `hotwallet.kaiching`, and each wrapped a delegate action from a different `*.users.kaiching` account. In that block the `*.users.kaiching` accounts appear only inside the delegate actions, never as signers. `sample-accounts.mjs` reads signers only, so this one block is all the evidence for that pattern. Distinct signers in the two equal samples moved much less (288 → 237). In transactions, the spring fall was one app's traffic stopping (relayed, in the block we checked), so NEAR's raw transaction count says little about its usage trend.

Reproduce with `node sample-accounts.mjs 300 --head 223809361`, which takes about 25 minutes at the free tier's 30 blocks a minute. The counts are in `data/raw/accounts-sample.json`, and the spot check is `curl https://mainnet.neardata.xyz/v0/block/188755667`.

**P3, NEAR Intents vs Relay on mainstream corridors (added 2026-11-10, after the lock; a REPORT, not scored).** T9 prices the agent's own two routes, and both start on Robinhood Chain, a young chain. P3 asks the same question on mainstream corridors between large chains. `quote-corridors.mjs` asks NEAR Intents' 1Click API (`dry: true`) and Relay's quote API for one quote per ticket ($100, $1,000, $10,000) on five corridors: Ethereum USDC → Base USDC, Arbitrum USDC → Base USDC, Ethereum ETH → Arbitrum ETH, Base ETH → Ethereum ETH and Ethereum USDC → BSC USDC. We chose the corridors on 11-10 after about ten dry $100 1Click probes. T9 had met 1Click's "$1,000 minimum" on Robinhood ETH → BSC BNB since 10-31, and the probes looked for where that limit applies (below). Ethereum USDC → BSC USDC is in the set on purpose, so the limit shows inside the comparison. The quote set was drawn after these choices. Nothing is deposited or signed.

- **Same input:** both APIs get the same raw amount, sized at 1Click's own token price.
- **Cost in token units:** each corridor moves one asset, so cost = 1 − amount out ÷ amount in. We do not use the APIs' USD figures, because each API prices the two sides itself. Relay's USD figures even gave negative costs on Base ETH → Ethereum ETH (−0.03% at $10,000; just below zero on Ethereum USDC → Base USDC too), which no bridge pays. On Ethereum USDC → BSC USDC the BSC side is Binance-Peg USDC, a different token, so reading that corridor in token units assumes it trades at par.
- **Expected output** on both sides, not the slippage minimum.
- **Origin gas left out of both:** the gas of the deposit transaction is in neither cost. Relay reports it ($0.0005 from Base to $0.02 from Ethereum in this set); 1Click does not.
- **One quote set:** quotes move within minutes (T9), so a rerun gives a nearby set, not the same one.

**Result (quoted 2026-11-10 03:24Z).** Every quote cost 0.010–0.23% of the amount. NEAR Intents was cheaper on 7 of the 14 tickets both venues quoted. At $100 it was cheaper on 3 of 4 (median 0.014% against Relay's 0.039%). At $10,000 the two medians are 0.018% and 0.020%, and Relay was cheaper on 3 of 5. The two large gaps run in opposite directions: on Ethereum ETH → Arbitrum ETH, 1Click charged 0.010–0.013% and Relay 0.05–0.07%; on Ethereum USDC → BSC USDC from $1,000 up, 1Click charged 0.020% and Relay 0.21%. Relay quoted 1–24 s to settle, 1Click 27–48 s.

**Where the "$1,000 minimum" applies.** The probes came first, before the corridors were chosen. A dry $100 quote from Ethereum USDC to Base USDC went through (0.016%), so the limit is not general. 1Click refused $100 into BSC (Ethereum ETH and Arbitrum ETH to BNB, Ethereum USDC to BSC USDC) and out of BSC (BSC USDC to Ethereum USDC), each with "Temporary swap limits: minimum swap amount is $1,000". It accepted $100 into Optimism and Polygon, which it reaches through the same HOT Omni bridge as BSC, and out of Robinhood Chain (to Ethereum and Base). The Arbitrum ETH → BNB and Robinhood → Base probes were run by hand and are not in the file. `quote-corridors.mjs` repeats six of these probes, so the recorded file carries them, and in the quote set itself 1Click refused only the $100 ticket into BSC. So the limit follows BSC. It is not set by the Robinhood origin, the bridge, or NEAR Intents as a whole. This narrows T9's second finding of 10-31 ("the minimum is per route") to one chain. These probes predate 2026-11-10 15:07Z: from that hour on, the hourly $30 Robinhood Chain ETH → BSC probe in `data/snapshots.jsonl` is accepted in every row that got an answer, so the BSC minimum no longer applies to the agent's route (P3's own quote date is in its row).

**Next to T9.** In the 2026-11-09 22:43Z quotes, the agent's own routes cost 0.17–0.95% through 1Click and 0.70–5.2% through Relay. That is roughly ten times the mainstream medians above (0.014–0.039%) or more, on either venue. T9's routes also swap one asset for another (ETH → BNB, USDG → NEAR) and P3's do not, so part of that gap is the swap. For this agent, where it moves money changes the price more than which of the two venues it uses.

Reproduce with `node quote-corridors.mjs` (about a minute; public APIs, no keys). The quotes are in `data/raw/corridor-quotes.json`; corridors and method are in `lib/corridors.mjs`.

**The protocol's own fee (added 2026-11-07, after the lock; a REPORT, not scored).** We tried to check Intents volume on chain through the contract's fee and found a different, smaller thing worth stating. `intents.near` charges `fee()` = 1 pip, i.e. 0.0001% (`Pips::MAX` = 1,000,000 in [near/intents](https://github.com/near/intents) at `32cd1f9`, `crates/primitives/fees/src/lib.rs`), on each side's token_in of a `token_diff` intent, and nothing on `transfer` intents (`contracts/defuse/core/src/intents/token_diff.rs`). ## Data limits (stated up front)

- **Intents volume comes from one pipeline: NEAR's own.** DefiLlama's `near-intents` volume adapter is a single query, `SELECT sum(volume_amount_usd) FROM dune.near.dataset_near_intents_metrics`, on a Dune dataset published by NEAR (DefiLlama/dimension-adapters `dexs/near-intents/index.ts`, read 2026-10-31). So when DefiLlama's September 2026 total ($4.80B) equals the September record NEAR reports, that is one pipeline agreeing with itself, not two sources. T1, T2, T7 and F1 depend on it. We did not re-derive volume from chain data: on chain, 1Click swaps settle mostly as transfers to solvers rather than swap events, and reconstructing "volume" would need definitions we cannot verify. The independent check we can make is on direction: over the 100 days NearBlocks publishes, DefiLlama's daily volume moves with NearBlocks' own daily count of `intents.near` transactions (r = 0.68 for daily log changes, 0.73 for log levels; T2 reports it). That is evidence the series tracks real activity, not a check of its dollar level.
- NearBlocks' `active_accounts` and `txns` are NearBlocks' definitions.
- NearBlocks' per-method counters for `intents.near` looked frozen on 10-31, so they are recorded but not used.
- The Agent Market job board and competitions need a login, so job prices and unfilled demand are not observable. Only delivered-job counts are.
- **Why small tickets cost more (T9).** Same-chain dry quotes on Robinhood Chain (ETH → USDG through 1Click, 2026-10-31 17:40Z) cost 1.96% at $5, 0.66% at $20 and 0.36% at $100. That fits a fixed ≈ $0.075 per swap plus ≈ 0.29% of the ticket. Of the 0.29%, 0.2% is 1Click's fee for callers without a partner key (HACKATHON.md Q-006). The fixed part behaves like the bridge's per-withdrawal cost. We did not see the split directly, so this is a fit to three points, not a fee schedule. It is consistent with the per-route result above. The hood USDG → NEAR route, which never withdraws back to Robinhood Chain, costs a flat ~0.3–0.7% even at $0.47, where a $0.075 fixed part alone would be 16%. So for agent-size tickets, what matters is whether the route ends with a bridge withdrawal, not the ticket size alone.
- **Agent activity is not labelled on either source.** The agent's own NEAR account (T8) appears nowhere in the Agent Market: all 527 listings identify the builder and owner by a platform UUID, and none has a field that links it to a NEAR account (the few `.near` names that appear are in free-text descriptions).
